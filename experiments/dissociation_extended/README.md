# Fragment separation at 50 and 100 Å

This extends the [3–20 Å diagnostic](../dissociation/) to 50 and 100 Å. For each of the 14 original training solutes, construct either a solute plus its first water **with other waters omitted**, or a solute plus its intact original environment. Translate the water or full environment away from the stationary solute. Keep internal fragment geometry and solute-centred probe points fixed. Recompute the polar features, averaged prior and frozen GLIDER head at every geometry.

**GLIDER's predicted amplitude grows at long separation.** Between 20 and 100 Å, its mean response-potential RMS rises from 0.564 to 1.540 mEh/e for the one-water system and from 0.925 to 2.843 mEh/e for the full cluster. The averaged prior instead falls from 0.195 to 0.113 and from 0.301 to 0.239 mEh/e, respectively. The zero-response limit applies to both two-fragment systems. These are predicted amplitudes on fixed solute-centred probes, not errors against new QM calculations. [View all Figure S7 values](../../figures/figure_S07/).

The [component diagnostic](posthoc_components.csv) contains 280 per-configuration rows across both separation sets. Its `prior`, `glider`, `without_reconciliation` and `reconciliation_*` potential-RMS columns are in mEh/e. The RMS of separate components does not add linearly to the RMS of their sum. Averaging 14 rows per system and distance shows the reconciliation component rising from 0.419 to 1.470 mEh/e for one water and from 0.670 to 2.708 mEh/e for the intact cluster between 20 and 100 Å. Omitting that component only at inference leaves 100 Å full-potential RMSs of 0.422 and 0.619 mEh/e. This identifies a major source of the growth, but it is a diagnostic, not a validated replacement model. A [one-solute QM check](../dissociation_qm/) now provides direct reference values for 20 of these geometries. **Paper:** workshop Fig. S7, journal Fig. 8c,d and Supplementary Section 11.4.
